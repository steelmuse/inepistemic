Critical Rationalism

This map is for readers already familiar with Critical Rationalism who want to see where inepistemic takes a familiar epistemic concept and sharpens, redirects, or recursively applies it. It is not a primer and it is not a reading order. Start from the distinction that already interests you, then follow the linked chapters.

The book's recurring Critical Rationalist question is:

What happens when an epistemology of problems, conjectures, criticism, and error correction is turned onto the singular person in whom those processes occur?

If you want a short path

For a first pass through the book's strongest Critical Rationalist movement, use:

00 - Universality → 08 - Criticism → 09 - Problems → 13 - Inexplicit → 17 - Arrows → 21 - Error → 29 - Authorities → 30 - Inepistemic

That path moves from epistemology applied personally, through criticism, problem creation, inexplicit knowledge, and the unexplained transitions between epistemic states, into corrigibility, authority, and the recursive criticism of the book's own epistemology.

For the fuller argument and the relations among these distinctions, use the longer Reader's Guide: Critical Rationalism.

Knowledge, truth, and justification

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Fallibilism Possibility of error / architecture of corrigibility. It is one thing to accept that any explanation may be wrong; another to preserve the people, records, alternatives, criticism, reversibility, and time through which the error can actually become visible. 12 - Justification · 21 - Error
Objective truth Truth / present possession of truth. The book retains truth as the standard against which error is possible while refusing to treat confidence, consensus, evidence, expertise, or felt intelligibility as a terminal warrant. 07 - Consilience · 12 - Justification
Anti-justificationism Rejecting justification in principle / reproducing justificatory structure in practice. A rejected foundation can return through confidence, consensus, authority, probability, expertise, or other representations that begin functioning as warrants. 12 - Justification · 29 - Authorities
Sources of knowledge Pedigree / criticism of the claim. The origin of an idea can matter causally without certifying its truth. The manuscript later extends this distinction from inherited knowledge to the ancestry of inventions and explanations. 00 - Universality · 18 - Unpredictability · 26 - Invention
Tradition and authority Knowledge-bearing inheritance / epistemic sovereignty. Traditions, experts, institutions, and inherited explanations may contain real knowledge without thereby becoming terminal courts beyond criticism. 03 - Societies · 29 - Authorities

Problems, criticism, and knowledge growth

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Problem situation Problem as starting point / problem as explanatory object. The epistemic cycle can begin with a problem while leaving open how that problem became salient, criticizable, or available in the first place. 09 - Problems
Criticism Error elimination / creative criticism. Criticism can do more than reject an existing conjecture; it can create a distinction, expose a hidden assumption, alter a criterion, or generate the successor problem. 08 - Criticism · 09 - Problems
P₁ → TS → EE → P₂ Useful schema / compressed arrows. Naming the epistemic states does not yet explain the transformations between them: why this conjecture appeared, why this criticism mattered, or how a new problem became visible. 17 - Arrows · 18 - Unpredictability
Error elimination Removing a candidate / changing the problem. A criticism may improve a solution inside a stable problem or transform the governing problem itself. Those are different epistemic events. 08 - Criticism · 09 - Problems · 23 - Understanding
Taking an explanation seriously Assenting to an explanation / allowing its consequences to travel. An explanation becomes epistemically active when its implications collide with other explanations and generate new problems. 07 - Consilience · 14 - Seriously

The singular person

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Universal epistemology Epistemology stated universally / epistemology applied to you. If problems, conjectures, criticism, and knowledge growth are universal, the singular person in whom they occur becomes an epistemic object requiring explanation. 00 - Universality · 15 - Personhood
Objective knowledge Public availability / singular reconstruction. The same objective knowledge can be available to many people without producing the same reconstruction, problem, or explanatory history in each. 03 - Societies · 04 - Solitude · 07 - Consilience
Knowledge about people Population regularity / singular causal explanation. A classification, measurement, trait, diagnosis, or statistical relation can contain real knowledge without containing the causal history of one particular person. 01 - Genetics · 02 - Populations · 03 - Societies
Explicit criticism Explicit knowledge / inexplicit participation in criticism. Recognition, expectation, skill, mismatch, rejection, or salience can affect epistemic change before the relevant knowledge has been formulated propositionally. 13 - Inexplicit · 14 - Seriously
Individual autonomy Independence / capacity to reconstruct and criticize. The book's concern is less isolation from inherited knowledge than whether inherited explanations can become problems for the singular person who encounters them. 04 - Solitude · 05 - Openness · 29 - Authorities

Openness, novelty, and unpredictability

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Growth of knowledge Unknown future content / open epistemic future. A present description can constrain future possibilities without already containing the genuinely new explanatory knowledge that may later be created. 05 - Openness · 18 - Unpredictability
Novel conjecture Ancestry / derivation. Once a new explanation exists, its influences and causal history may be reconstructed without that reconstruction amounting to a derivation of the explanatory transition in advance. 18 - Unpredictability · 26 - Invention
Creativity Novel output / explanatory creativity. Novelty of an artifact is not yet an explanation of the new abstraction, relation, or problem under which the artifact became intelligible as a solution. 16 - Mystery · 26 - Invention
Mystery Unexplained transition / exemption from explanation. The sudden appearance of a conjecture, value, or invention can mark an explanatory gap without licensing a causal discontinuity or a protected mystery. 16 - Mystery · 17 - Arrows

Social knowledge, coordination, and authority

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Open society Permission to criticize / preservation of future criticism. Openness is not exhausted by allowing criticisms that are already formulable; arrangements also affect whether new criticisms and successor problems can become possible. 05 - Openness · 21 - Error · 28 - Motion
Institutions Functional coordination / collective epistemic subject. Institutions can preserve knowledge and coordinate action without thereby becoming a single mind that knows or reconstructs as a person does. 04 - Solitude · 10 - Coordination
Authority Useful authority relation / terminal epistemic authority. Expertise, responsibility, office, or inherited success may justify present coordination without conferring permanent sovereignty over future criticism. 03 - Societies · 29 - Authorities
Policy or rule Representation / causal input. Once a classification or explanation is embodied in a rule, it no longer merely describes a person or population; it begins changing the problem environment it was originally meant to represent. 03 - Societies · 20 - Resources · 29 - Authorities
Corrective institution Control / error-correction surface. A system can constrain harmful action while preserving broad criticism, alternatives, records, and reversibility; control and corrigibility are not the same variable. 21 - Error · 29 - Authorities

Objective knowledge, interfaces, and AI

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Objective knowledge Static artifact / interactive objective knowledge. Objective knowledge can become technologically responsive to the problem you are presently forming without that responsiveness by itself establishing an independent epistemic subject. 22 - Interfaces · 24 - AI
Tool Stored knowledge / responsive objective surface. A system can return candidate explanations, decompositions, criticisms, or code early enough to alter the problem situation in which later knowledge is created. 13 - Inexplicit · 24 - AI
Understanding Complete reconstruction / criticizable abstraction. Useful understanding need not require possession of every lower-level detail if the relevant explanatory layer can be criticized and deeper structure reopened when it becomes problematic. 22 - Interfaces · 23 - Understanding
Machine performance Transformation of objective knowledge / explanatory creativity. Powerful search, generation, recombination, memory, or optimization does not by itself settle the ontology of the epistemic subject creating a new explanation. 23 - Understanding · 24 - AI · 26 - Invention
Memory Stored states / epistemic history. Preserving outputs or conversation history is different from preserving the genealogy of problems, criticisms, rejected conjectures, and changed criteria that makes later states intelligible. 17 - Arrows · 24 - AI · 28 - Motion

Values, resources, and corrigibility

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Values Governing commitment / compressed explanation. A value can carry explanatory work compactly while remaining fallible; the danger appears when the compression can no longer be reopened. 19 - Values
Resources Material capacity / epistemic reach. Resources change which problems, experiments, criticisms, and communities are practically reachable without determining the epistemic direction a person will take. 20 - Resources
Error correction Abstract commitment / practical surface. Error correction requires more than admitting fallibility: criticism must have somewhere to occur, alternatives must survive, and consequential mistakes must remain discoverable. 21 - Error
Freedom to criticize Current permission / conditions for future criticism. Preserving criticism includes preserving enough epistemic movement for criticisms that do not yet exist to become possible later. 21 - Error · 28 - Motion
No final authority Anti-authoritarian principle / removable ruler. The manuscript turns the principle back onto its own explanations: any governing abstraction, including its own epistemology, must remain available to criticism and replacement. 29 - Authorities · 30 - Inepistemic

The recursive destination

Familiar Critical Rationalist concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Critical Rationalism as doctrine Epistemology possessed / epistemology in motion. The book's destination is not merely being able to state an epistemology, but allowing it to alter the problems, explanations, institutions, technologies, and personal knowledge through which you live and build. 14 - Seriously · 30 - Inepistemic
Criticism of other explanations Criticism turned recursively on the framework itself. The same standards used to criticize population models, institutions, AI, values, or authority must remain available against the manuscript's own distinctions. 29 - Authorities · 30 - Inepistemic
