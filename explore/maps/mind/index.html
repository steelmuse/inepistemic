Mind

This map is for readers coming from cognitive science, neuroscience, philosophy of mind, consciousness research, psychology, agency, creativity, computation, or related accounts of mind who want to see where Inepistemic makes a specifically epistemic distinction. It is not a reading order. Start from a concept already familiar to you, then follow the linked chapters.

The manuscript's recurring question for this audience is:

What changes when a mind is described not only by its present states or mechanisms, but also by the problems, criticisms, reconstructions, and explanatory transitions through which its knowledge changes?

If you want a short path

For a first pass through the epistemic mind argument, the most concentrated sequence is:

05 - Openness → 08 - Criticism → 09 - Problems → 13 - Inexplicit → 15 - Personhood → 17 - Arrows → 18 - Unpredictability → 25 - Taboo

That path moves from present knowledge and open future, through criticism, problem activation, inexplicit knowledge, and self-explanation, into epistemic transitions, future explanations, and models of other minds.

For the fuller argument and the relations among these distinctions, use the longer Reader's Guide: The Epistemic Mind.

Description, history, and the singular person

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Population structure / individual differences Between-person structure / within-person causal and epistemic history. Knowledge about variation across people is a different explanatory object from the history through which one person's knowledge changes. 01 - Genetics · 02 - Populations
Current mental state Present state / open epistemic future. A present description can constrain or predict without thereby containing explanatory knowledge that has not yet been created. 05 - Openness · 18 - Unpredictability
Self-model Self-description / epistemic completion. A person can create increasingly explicit accounts of themselves without arriving at a final account that closes further explanation. 15 - Personhood
Model of another person Reconstruction / substitution. A model of another person can be informative without becoming identical to a person who may continue creating knowledge beyond it. 25 - Taboo

Problems, salience, and criticism

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Problem representation Available problem / active problem. A contradiction, task, or question can be objectively available without becoming a live problem for this person. 09 - Problems
Salience / attention What is available / what becomes epistemically active. Retained knowledge changes which distinctions, mismatches, and conflicts can become problems next. 05 - Openness · 09 - Problems
Error signal / mismatch Error detection / explanatory criticism. Detecting failure does not by itself specify which assumption, relation, abstraction, or criterion should change. 08 - Criticism · 09 - Problems
Conflict representation Carrier / reconstruction. A text, model, memory, or machine can preserve incompatible structures without those structures yet becoming a live epistemic conflict for a person. 07 - Consilience · 08 - Criticism
Learning history Past states / problem-world formation. The epistemically relevant history concerns how prior knowledge changes which problems can become visible and active next. 09 - Problems

Implicit, explicit, and conscious availability

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Implicit / tacit knowledge Knowledge participating in cognition / explicit object later created to represent it. Knowledge can operate in recognition, skill, expectation, salience, or judgment before it is propositionally explicit. 13 - Inexplicit
Introspection / report Explicitation / transcription. Making something explicit need not retrieve a fully formed hidden sentence; the explicit representation may itself be newly created. 13 - Inexplicit
Conscious access Availability / availability to criticism. The manuscript's interest is not a theory of consciousness but what changes epistemically when content can enter explicit criticism and reconstruction. 05 - Openness
Self-report Representation / final causal account. An explicit report can become a new criticizable object without being a complete explanation of the knowledge it represents. 13 - Inexplicit · 15 - Personhood

Abstraction, explanation, and experienced reality

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Representation Usable representation / causal structure represented. An abstraction can be useful precisely because it suppresses detail, without becoming identical to the structure it compresses. 06 - Abstractions
Prior knowledge shaping perception Inherited abstraction / experienced reality. A representation can become so functionally transparent that it no longer appears to the person as a representation at all. 06 - Abstractions
Having an explanation Possession / reach. An explanation can be possessed without its consequences being allowed to reorganize other parts of the person's problem situation. 14 - Seriously
Understanding oneself Explanation / recursive return. Once an explanation of yourself becomes objective knowledge, it can return to your own problem situation and become material for further criticism. 14 - Seriously · 15 - Personhood

Creativity, insight, and transition

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Insight / sudden thought Sudden appearance / causal discontinuity. An idea can appear abruptly at the level available to report without that appearance establishing a break in causality. 16 - Mystery
Search Search within a represented problem / transformation of problem and criteria. A new solution may require not just searching candidates but changing the problem or how candidates are judged. 16 - Mystery · 09 - Problems
Successive cognitive states State description / transition explanation. Naming the before and after states does not explain the transformation between them. 17 - Arrows
Temporal sequence Next event / epistemic direction. What occurs next in time is not necessarily the transition that reorganizes what can be thought next. 17 - Arrows
Novel explanation Ancestry / derivation. Reconstructing the influences and history of an explanation after it exists does not by itself derive the creative transition that produced it. 18 - Unpredictability

Prediction and the open future

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Prediction Predicting aspects of the present organization / possessing future explanatory knowledge. These are different achievements. 05 - Openness · 18 - Unpredictability
Unknown future state Unknown value / not-yet-created explanation. A future explanation is not merely an unknown present variable if the explanation itself has not yet been created. 18 - Unpredictability
Physical determinacy Epistemic non-possession / physical indeterminacy. The manuscript's claim concerns present possession and explanatory novelty; it does not require a claim that the physical future is indeterminate. 18 - Unpredictability
Stable model of a person Informative model / boundary on possible futures. A useful present model need not be treated as exhaustive of the person's future explanatory states. 05 - Openness · 18 - Unpredictability

Other minds and external knowledge

Familiar concept Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
External artifact Stored knowledge / returning epistemic condition. Objective knowledge can leave one person, become embodied externally, and later re-enter another person's problem situation. 00 - Universality · 24 - AI
Extended or coupled cognition Causal participation / identity of epistemic subject. External systems can participate causally in knowledge creation without that participation alone settling what the epistemic subject is. 04 - Solitude · 24 - AI
Love / interpersonal understanding Reconstruction / continuing source of explanation. Another person can remain an evolving epistemic source beyond any current model of them. 25 - Taboo
Memory of another person Surviving objective traces / continuing person. Preserved knowledge about a person is not equivalent to the ongoing source of future explanations that existed while the person lived. 25 - Taboo

What this map is for

The map does not claim that cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, philosophy of mind, or consciousness research have overlooked these phenomena. Its narrower purpose is to expose the manuscript's epistemic cuts clearly enough that readers can compare them with whatever mechanistic, computational, dynamical, phenomenological, developmental, or biological account they already use.

The recurring distinction is between describing what a mind is like now and explaining the epistemic relations through which its knowledge changes.
