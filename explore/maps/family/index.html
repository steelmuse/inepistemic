Family & Friends

This map is for readers interested in how Inepistemic bears on family, friendship, love, familiarity, care, and knowing another person over time. It is not a reading order. Start from an experience already familiar to you, then follow the linked chapters.

The recurring question for this audience is:

What changes when the people you know most intimately are treated as continuing creators of knowledge rather than as the people your accumulated models say they are?

If you want a short path

For a first pass through the family-and-friends argument, the most concentrated sequence is:

04 - Solitude → 05 - Openness → 06 - Abstractions → 13 - Inexplicit → 17 - Arrows → 25 - Taboo → 28 - Motion → 29 - Authorities

That path moves from shared history and singular reconstruction, through familiarity, inexplicit change, and unexplained transitions, into love, recursive influence, and authority.

For the fuller argument and the relations among these distinctions, use the longer Reader's Guide: Family and Friends.

Family role Useful compression / ontology of the person. “The responsible one,” “the difficult one,” or “the practical one” can coordinate expectations without becoming the person themselves. 06 - Abstractions

Change and self-explanation

Familiar experience Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
“They must have known this all along.” Inexplicit knowledge / later explicit reconstruction. A later explanation need not be a transcript of an already completed earlier thought. 09 - Problems · 13 - Inexplicit
Self-understanding Self-explanation / self-completion. Turning explanation back onto yourself can create new knowledge without producing a final inventory of yourself. 15 - Personhood
Talking something through Report / causal intervention. Making an explanation objective can alter the problem situation of the person doing the explaining. 14 - Seriously · 28 - Motion
A person changing their story about themselves Contradiction / recursive reconstruction. A later explanation can reorganize the significance of earlier events without making either account automatically final. 13 - Inexplicit · 15 - Personhood

Before, after, and the missing arrow

Familiar experience Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
“I remember when they changed.” Before-and-after states / explanation of the transition. Knowing the chronology does not by itself explain what criticism, distinction, or new problem made the change possible. 17 - Arrows
A sudden change Sudden appearance / causal discontinuity. A change can appear abrupt without its causal history being absent. 16 - Mystery · 17 - Arrows
A long-running unresolved concern Recurring behavior / persistent problem. Visible episodes may be states produced by a problem that only later becomes explicit enough to reconstruct. 09 - Problems · 13 - Inexplicit
A later explanation of an earlier life Retrospective reconstruction / derivation. A later account can explain more without proving that the explanation was already present in the earlier person. 13 - Inexplicit · 18 - Unpredictability

Love, projection, and other minds

Familiar experience Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Knowing someone you love Reconstruction / continuing person. The better the model becomes, the easier it can be to forget that the person continues creating knowledge beyond it. 25 - Taboo
Projection Model corrected by the person / person corrected to fit the model. A representation becomes dangerous when departures are treated only as defects in the person represented. 25 - Taboo
Confidence in a relationship Reliable expectation / possession of the future person. Prediction can be useful without closing the possibility that the other person's future knowledge changes the model. 05 - Openness · 18 - Unpredictability
Memory after death Surviving objective traces / continuing source of explanations. Preserved knowledge can remain while the person as a source of future knowledge is gone. 25 - Taboo

Care, children, and authority

Familiar experience Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Wanting to prepare a child for the future Known needs / future problems not yet created. A child's later problems and the knowledge needed to solve them cannot all be inventoried in advance. 18 - Unpredictability · 30 - Inepistemic
Care and intervention Knowing more / permanent epistemic sovereignty. Greater knowledge or responsibility can matter without making the explanation under which you act final. 29 - Authorities

Protecting someone Constraint / completion of the person. Acting under uncertainty does not require treating the current model of the person as exhaustive. 21 - Error · 29 - Authorities

Family and friendship as causal environments

Familiar experience Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
Giving advice Supplying an answer / changing a problem situation. An explanation can become part of what another person later reconstructs, criticizes, or rejects. 03 - Societies · 14 - Seriously
Opening an opportunity Known benefit / epistemic trajectory. Access to people, tools, communities, and problems can create later criticism and knowledge that could not be specified in advance. 20 - Resources
Restricting someone for their own good Prevented action / prevented trajectory. A restriction can remove a known danger while also removing future problem histories that never become observable. 20 - Resources · 21 - Error
Mutual influence Two fixed selves interacting / recursive change. Each person's explanations and criticism can alter the problem situation encountered by the other on the next pass. 28 - Motion

The reciprocal relation

Familiar idea Distinction sharpened in the book Chapters
“I know you.” Model / continuing epistemic source. Knowledge of another person remains answerable to the person who can still surprise, criticize, and change it. 25 - Taboo
“You know me.” Being represented / remaining open. The same openness you claim for yourself applies to the people whose lives you explain. 05 - Openness · 25 - Taboo
Knowing each other Mutual models / reciprocal openness. Neither side can demand openness for itself while treating the other as a completed character. 25 - Taboo · 29 - Authorities
The shortest version of this reading path is reciprocal:

The people closest to you are among the people you know best. That makes your models richer, not final. They remain capable of changing the explanations through which you know them, just as you remain capable of changing theirs.
